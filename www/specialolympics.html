Special Olympics Belgium

Information Architecture & UX Redesign

Special Olympics Belgium serves a wide range of audiences, including athletes, volunteers, donors, sponsors and family members. The existing website contained valuable information, but users often struggled to find relevant content due to an outdated structure and unclear navigation.

The goal of this academic project was to redesign the website's information architecture and validate navigation decisions through user-centered research methods before creating the final interface.

Challenge

The existing website had grown over time and no longer reflected how users expected information to be organised. Rather than jumping directly into visual redesigns, the project focused on understanding how users searched for information and how the content structure could be improved.

Research & Discovery

To better understand user needs, I first identified the primary target groups and their goals.

This helped define the research questions that would guide the redesign:

  • What information do visitors expect to find first?
  • Which content is difficult to locate?
  • How do users naturally group information?
  • Which navigation labels feel most intuitive?

Card Sorting

To create a structure based on users' mental models instead of internal organisational categories, I conducted a card sorting exercise. The results revealed consistent content groupings across participants.

Categories such as About Us, Sports, Get Involved, What We Do and Support Us emerged naturally.

Most Common Content Categories

The results showed strong agreement among participants, particularly around the categories About Us, Sports & Events, Get Involved and What We Do. These recurring patterns formed the basis of the redesigned navigation structure.

Key Findings

After defining the new information architecture through card sorting, I validated the navigation using tree testing and one-click testing.

Participants completed tasks related to athlete registration, volunteering, donations, events and organisation information. The following findings informed the final navigation refinements.

01

Donation terminology caused confusion

4 out of 5 participants successfully located the donation section. One participant experienced hesitation due to unclear terminology. To improve clarity, one of the "Support Us" labels was renamed to "Make a Donation", making the action more explicit.

02

Youth Board Run Club was expected elsewhere

Two participants looked for the Youth Board Run Club under "Get Involved" rather than "What We Do". This highlighted a mismatch between the website structure and users' mental models.

03

Core tasks were generally successful

Most participants were able to complete important tasks such as finding volunteer opportunities, athlete registration and organisation information, providing confidence in the overall navigation structure.

Navigation Refinements

Based on tree testing and one-click testing results, several navigation labels and content groupings were refined before moving into the final prototype. Users expected the "Support Us" section to immediately present available support options.

For donations, instead of using a dropdown navigation with multiple subpages, the final version links directly to a dedicated support page where all donation and sponsorship opportunities are grouped together.

Design Improvements

The redesigned structure was tested with users through usability testing and one-click tests. These findings directly informed further iterations of the navigation structure and page hierarchy.

01

Athlete Registration Visibility

Several participants struggled to locate athlete registration. To improve discoverability, a dedicated homepage section with a clear call-to-action was introduced.

02

Event Discoverability

Participants expected upcoming events to be easier to find. A dedicated Calendar section was added alongside a homepage overview of upcoming events.

03

Donation Clarity

Donation and sponsorship pathways were rewritten using clearer language to reduce confusion between individual donations and corporate sponsorships.

Finale Outcome

The final information architecture reduced ambiguity in the navigation structure and improved the discoverability of key tasks such as athlete registration, volunteering and donations. User testing validated that the revised navigation better matched users' expectations and mental models.
